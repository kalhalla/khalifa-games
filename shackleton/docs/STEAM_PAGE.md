## 1. Art direction (for all key art)

**One image to build everything from:** a single piece of key art, cropped for each capsule.

- **Scene:** low angle across the regolith. The toppled ship lies on its side across the middle distance, KHAL stacked up its flank and one landing leg pointing at the sky. The astronaut stands small in the foreground, seen from behind or three-quarters, helmet turned towards the ship. The three robots stand near him: L-07 yellow, M-08 red, P-03 blue. Earth hangs low in a pure black sky.
- **Light:** a low sun from one side, about 17°. Long, hard, black shadows and no fill light. The robots' eyes are the only glow.
- **Look:** the game's own. Low-poly, flat-shaded, clean silhouettes; not painterly realism. A render from the game, recomposed and touched up, is a valid route.
- **Palette:** black sky, bone-grey regolith, cream robot bodies, with small accents of yellow, red, blue and the ice's pale blue. Keep it mostly monochrome so the colours read.
- **Mood:** quiet, lonely, tender. Not action.

**Logo (the title):** SHACKLETON in a wide-tracked geometric sans, capitals, thin to medium weight. Think mission patch or spacecraft stencil, not sci-fi chrome. Supply one white version and one dark version, each on a transparent background. An optional mark: a small circle of five figures holding hands, the M-08 sculpture from the script.

**Brief for an artist or an image tool:** "Low-poly flat-shaded lunar scene, low sun, hard black shadows, black sky with Earth low on the horizon. A toppled white spacecraft on its side, half sunk in grey regolith, one landing leg pointing up, KHAL lettered vertically on its hull. A small astronaut in a white suit in the foreground with three small boxy robots (cream bodies; yellow, red and blue trim; glowing square eyes). Quiet, lonely, hopeful."

## 4. Store text (draft)

**Name:** Shackleton

**Short description** (Steam limit 300 characters; this is 240):
> You wake beside a toppled ship in a crater at the Moon's south pole, low on oxygen, with no memory. Three small robots have kept working while you slept. Rebuild a way home with them — and remember who is still calling for you.

**About this game:**

> **Two days ago, the landing went wrong.**
>
> You wake face-down in the regolith of Shackleton crater with ten minutes of oxygen and no memory of how you got here. The ship that carried you lies on its side. Three robots — L-07, M-08 and P-03 — are still at work around the wreck, as if nothing has happened.
>
> They don't say much. They're glad you're awake.
>
> **Explore a lonely, beautiful crater.** Low sun, long black shadows, ice hidden in the dark. Every trip is measured in oxygen; every repair pushes the edge of where you can go.
>
> **Solve hands-on puzzles.** Re-route oxygen manifolds, free trapped parts, coax a frozen ice pocket into giving you air. Each fix rebuilds the small Flyer that might lift you over the rim.
>
> **Work with three robot friends.** Ask them questions on your wrist pad. They answer in a word or two, point the way, and look out for you more than they'll admit.
>
> **Remember, one piece at a time.** Fragments of your life return as you work — and a faint voice on the radio keeps calling your name.
>
> A short, quiet story about memory, family and getting home.

**Feature bullets** (if the template has a list):
- A hand-made lunar crater to explore, with oxygen as your range
- Physical puzzles built into the wreck and the landscape
- Three expressive robot companions you can question
- A story told in memories and radio static, not cutscenes
- Low-poly visuals, hard lunar light

**Tags** (Steam allows up to 20; most important first):
Exploration · Atmospheric · Puzzle · Story Rich · Space · Sci-fi · Robots · Adventure · Emotional · Narrative · Third Person · Singleplayer · Indie · Low-poly · Survival · Walking Simulator · Short · 3D · Relaxing · Cute

Drop "Relaxing" and "Cute" if they feel wrong; "Survival" is light here (oxygen only).

**Content descriptors:** none needed (no violence, no mature themes beyond loss).

**Supported languages:** English (interface and subtitles). The robot tones and Michel's radio voice are fine; every line is also on screen as text.
